## 1. TL;DR

- **The Theme in one sentence:** a night-navy Gotham under the Bat-Signal on the clouds, Bat-Clawd in violet satin and a lavender suit doing the Batusi, a narrator who promises more tomorrow, and a POW! balloon that pops beside the stamp when something needs you.
- **Display name: SAME BAT-TIME.** The serial's promise, cut before "Channel" (ANCIENT GRACE's word). Credit (`film`): "the 1966 Batman series" (no-break spaces inside the title, as BECOME A BAT's credit does). Alternatives: **BATUSI** (one word, like VENGEANCE), **POW!**.
- **What the brief got half wrong, measured.** The animated opening is not navy: its fields are flat green, magenta, orange and maroon [01]–[08]. The navy is in three other places: the dusk cast cards of the opening [09] [10] (h 256–276), the closing-credits sky [18] [19] (`#13487b`, h 275) and the live-action Bat-Signal on night clouds [17] (h 261–273). The ground follows those.
- **Ground: G1, the credits navy,** `#040e21` / `#011c3a` / `#012a54` (h 280, surface C* 22.6). Nearest: BLUE COLLAR 9.3 / 11.3, WATCHFUL PROTECTOR 10.1 / 11.1, SERIOUS HOUSE 13.3 / **9.3** (raised). G2, the cliffhanger card's ultramarine [34], is the alternative.
- **Alarm: three real options, one recommended.** Measured first: **on this navy no red passes 4.5:1 without turning orange.** The Batphone's red [51] [52] (`#e42f10`–`#f63d1d`, h 41) reads 3.2–3.8:1 on the raised ground; the first red that passes, `#fc5936`, is 8.4 from Clawd, 4.9 from SERIOUS HOUSE's scarlet and 6.3 from TAS's red sky.
  - **V, recommended: the satin's violet,** `#bb76ff` lit, `#8a3fe0` fill. The cowl's sheen on the film's satin [38] (`#826cc9`, h 303) and the cape [26] (`#4a337e`, h 306), lifted. 39.9 from Clawd; the nearest alarm is TAS's *fallback* B at 10.9; every other alarm, ink, suit and belt colour of every Theme and draft is 17.9 or more away.
  - **R, the Batphone red** `#fc5936`: the most faithful meaning (the phone rings when Gordon needs Batman). Costs above.
  - **G, the SFX green** `#3fd18a` (KERSPLOOSH [41]): 10.5 from SERIOUS HOUSE's working green, a third green claimant, and green reads as "OK". Listed, not recommended.
- **Inks:** waiting is the **SFX green** `#38c886` [41] [27] (11.7 from SERIOUS HOUSE's working green, 14.3 from the games' pale green); working is **Bone** `#f2e8ec`, the Signal's pale light [18].
- **The classic suit, confronted (§8.1).** West's suit is TAS's and HOLIDAY's family. At 54px what tells his apart is **hue**: violet satin cape and cowl (`#4a3480`, h 306) and a lavender suit (`#837b9c`, h 301), against TAS's cobalt (h 289) and blue-grey (h 283) and HOLIDAY's teal (h 244–257): 10.1–15.9 ΔE00. The parts that are his (painted brows, satin sheen, the oval without a rim, the square buckle) are at or under the 1-unit floor. The yellow is the same as TAS's (oval 2.9, belt 3.0). Three options for the jury, with what TAS and HOLIDAY give up.
- **POW! is not a Lexicon word.** It gives none of the five alerts' meanings. It is a **balloon beside a clear stamp word**: a starburst that pops once on the notice card when an alert arrives, in the alarm colour (engine ask). Lexicon: **Case · BATPHONE · MAY I? · FOILED · WHAT NOW? · NEW CLUE · HANGING**, all measured in Luckiest Guy at 11px: PERMISSION 88.4 / 101, the widest word (CASE OPENED) 93.6 / 101, every room passes.
- **Voice: the serial narrator.** Breathless, earnest, third person: "Meanwhile…", "stately Wayne Manor", one "same Bat-time" a session. No anchor, no "live", no static, no "Channel" (ANCIENT GRACE). Comedy is camp played straight; §6 ends with where it stops, for Lego Batman.
- **Signature: the Batusi** [20]–[22]. Held step W1: two gloves beside the eyes, the V across them (the white eyes stay clear), elbows out to x −0.6 and 16.6 at eye height. At 54px: a stand with two open arm loops beside the head, 3.6 units past the body each side. No shipped held step has anything beside the head (`boards-west/outlines.png`).
- **Type:** **Luckiest Guy** (Apache-2.0, free per the Wave 2 jury) for the wordmark and stamps, after the closing-credits caps [19]; **Bowlby One SC** (OFL) for headlines and the dateline, after the cliffhanger cards [31]–[34].
- **Atmosphere A1: clouds over the Signal** [17]: soft flat cloud banks drift sideways across a navy sky, the 1966 Signal pale behind them. An option on the `smoke` painter (wind, flat puffs) plus a still Signal.
- **Engine asks:** the balloon; the clouds option and still Signal; an emblem plate with no rim that never lights (shared with TAS's ask); Luckiest Guy and Bowlby One SC; nothing for the viewBox.

## 3. Visual identity and sources

What makes it recognisable, each from a source:

1. **Camp, played straight.** The show is "intentionally campy and tongue-in-cheek", and its characters "take the absurd situations very seriously" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_(TV_series))). Read: [43] (a bookcase labelled "ACCESS TO BATCAVE VIA BATPOLES"), [11] [12] (consoles labelled like a lab).
2. **The narrator and the cliffhanger.** Executive producer William Dozier narrated, uncredited, and ended the first part of each story with "Tune in tomorrow – same Bat-time, same Bat-channel!" (same page). Read: the cards [31]–[34] print the narrator's questions in yellow capitals over a frozen frame.
3. **Words on screen in the fights.** "POW!", "BAM!", "ZONK!" appear "superimposed on the screen" (same page). Read: full-frame cards [27]–[30] [37] and bursts over the action [04] [08] [13]–[15], in red, orange, green and light blue.
4. **Tilted cameras.** "Cameras were purposely placed out of level with the set", most often in the villains' lairs (same page). Used here as the stamps' tilt (§5).
5. **The Batusi.** It "first appeared in the premiere episode", "Hi Diddle Riddle", and again in "The Pharaoh's in a Rut"; a horizontal V of two fingers of both hands drawn across the eyes, the eyes between the fingers ([Wikipedia, Batusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batusi)). Read: [20]–[22], [25].
6. **The Batphone.** Police officials "gaze reverently at the Batphone" and Alfred answers it at Wayne Manor ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_(TV_series))). Read: a red phone under a glass dome that lights [51] [52].
7. **The suit.** "A skintight blue-and-grey costume" (same page). Read: a lavender-grey body; cowl, cape, gloves, trunks and boots in one violet-blue satin with a light sheen; painted pale brows and a nose line on the cowl; short ears [38] [47]; a yellow oval with a wide black bat and no rim [16] [21]; a yellow belt with a large square buckle [21] [48]; a cape to mid-calf with deep points at the hem [40] [46].
8. **Pop colour in the titles.** The animated opening is flat fields of green, magenta, orange and maroon [01]–[08]; its night is only in the cast cards [09] [10] and the closing credits [18] [19].

Home media: Warner Bros. released the whole series on Blu-ray and DVD on 11 November 2014, under licence from 20th Century Fox, after years of rights questions between DC/Warner, Fox, Greenway and ABC ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_(TV_series))). [35] [36] are from Warner's own trailer for it.

### 4.2 The Alarm color

**Why not red, measured.** The show's alarm is the red Batphone [51] [52], and red is the colour of most of its fight cards [14] [28] [29]. On G1 the Batphone's reds read 3.21:1 (`#e42f10`) and 3.82:1 (`#f63d1d`) on `--raised`. A red at h 41 needs L* 60 to pass 4.5:1 on `--raised` and `--notice-hover-top`; the first one is `#fc5936`: 8.4 from Clawd, 4.9 from SERIOUS HOUSE's `--signal-hot`, 6.3 from TAS's red sky A. Every red from h 20 to h 60 that passes lands under 10 from Clawd or from a taken red. VENGEANCE ships `#e3121b` only because its grounds are near-black. **On a navy ground no red passes contrast without becoming orange.** The yellow of the oval and of the cliffhanger cards is barred (PALE MOONLIGHT's gold, BLUE COLLAR's lemon: `#f7f12d` is 4.4 from it), and the pinks of the opening [02] are taken.

**Why V.** It is the suit's own colour under studio light: the cowl and cape satin sit at h 299–310 in every live-action sample [16] [26] [36] [38], and the cartoon cape at h 294 [03]. It is the only hue left with room: 17.9 or more from every alarm, ink, suit and belt of every shipped, paused and drafted Theme but TAS's fallback, 39.9 from Clawd. Its costs, measured:
- **The cape at rest is the alarm's hue family.** Cape `#4a3480` (h306, L* 28) to `--signal-hot` is 33.5, to `--signal` 17.5. This is PALE MOONLIGHT's unlit-brass pattern (an unlit relative of the alarm at rest, Wave 1 §10.6) and TAS's C1b. Per the Costume Rule the rim on cowl and cape is a neutral (Ash at 30%), never violet, and the aura shows only when something needs you. **Decide on screen** whether a lit violet disc beside a violet Bat-Clawd reads as one alarm.
- **Beside BLUE COLLAR in the picker.** Its ground is violet (h 301); this alarm is 48.9 from its surface, and the grounds differ by 9.3 / 11.3 (§4.4). Lit, our disc is violet; theirs is lemon.
- **A violet alarm is cooler than red.** It is the hottest colour in this palette (the next is the waiting green), which is what the Alarm color rule asks.

## 6. Voice

**Frame: the serial narrator.** A breathless, earnest man who sets the scene ("Meanwhile, at stately Wayne Manor…"), asks what will happen, and promises the answer tomorrow. Third person, present tense, exclamation marks rationed. He never speaks as Batman and never as a newsreader.

**The lines with the neighbours.** ANCIENT GRACE is TV *news*: an anchor, a panel, "live", static, Channel, Dead air. This Theme uses none of those words; it is TV *drama*, and its voice is the storyteller's, not the reporter's. TAS speaks in silent title cards; this one speaks in narration and cliffhanger questions. The only television word here is in the one catchphrase, used once a session, and "Channel" is never a Lexicon word.

Sample lines (P = ours; the quoted phrases are short):
- Intro tagline: **"Meanwhile, in Gotham City…"** (P)
- Empty state, all clear: **"All quiet at stately Wayne Manor."** ("stately Wayne Manor", three words of the narrator's.) *Quiet* means all clear only.
- No cases: "No cases tonight. The Batpoles are idle." (P)
- Quiet Signal tooltip: "All quiet at stately Wayne Manor." Lit: "The Batphone is ringing." (P)
- Night Report name: **"Tonight's episode"**; dateline `MEANWHILE · 21:40` (`datelineOf('MEANWHILE')`); case notes heading: "Meanwhile…" (P)
- Report signature line when something waits: "Will Claude get an answer?" (P)
- Report close, something still waiting: **"Same Bat-time tomorrow!"** — the catchphrase, cut before "Channel", once a session. Nothing waiting: "And so, another night's work is done." (P)
- Waiting sentence: "Claude waits. What will you do?" (P)
- Stalled sentence: "Left hanging. Nothing new from Claude for a while." (P)
- Settings kicker: "Utility belt"; Atmosphere switch: "Clouds" ("Night clouds over Gotham behind the cases"). (P)
- On the perch (`mascot.watching`): "Bat-Clawd doing the Batusi". (P)
- The labels device: the settings title reads **"Bat-Settings"**, as the Batcave labels its gadgets [43]. One "Bat-" label per screen at most. (P)

**Where the comedy stops.** This Theme's comedy is **camp played straight**, from 1966. The jokes come from the situation and the decor: everything labelled, a dance in a nightclub, a balloon that says POW!, a narrator who takes a stalled build as seriously as a death trap. Nobody in it knows it is funny. Batman is courteous, square and modest; he never brags, never mocks the user, never winks at the camera, never says he is the best. It is 1960s pop: primary colours, onomatopoeia, alliteration, the cliffhanger; no modern slang, no references to other Batmen, no jokes about being a Theme in an app, no fourth wall. **Lego Batman starts where Batman knows he is the joke**: self-parody, the huge ego, the hero who boasts and sulks, meta jokes about his own franchise (the 2017 film even has Alfred name the Batusi as a memory). West is sincere; Lego is self-aware. If a line needs Batman to be vain or ironic about himself, it is Lego's; if it needs him to be earnest about something absurd, it is West's.

## 7. Lexicon

**POW! is not a Lexicon word.** A tired developer reading POW!, BAM! or ZAP! on a stamp gets no standard meaning: not a permission, not an error, not a question, not news. It reads as "something happened", at an unknown severity ([`jury-wave-2.md`](jury-wave-2.md) §4's test). So the balloons are **decoration beside a clear word**:
- **The balloon (engine ask):** when an alert lands, a starburst about 34×22px in `--balloon` with POW! (Permission, Error), BAM! (Waiting, New reply) or ZAP! (Stalled) in `--balloon-ink`, Luckiest Guy, pops beside the stamp on the notice card for three steps of the 8 fps clock (grow, hold, fade). The stamp beside it carries the meaning. Never in the Night Report margin or the watch strip, never in a toast or the tray, nothing under reduced motion. POW! measures 46.9px at 11px, so a balloon of that size fits the notice card's 272px beside any stamp.
- **The lit disc** carries a burst (§9), and **Bat-Clawd's alarmed pose** stands in front of one (§8.3).
- An alternative, if the balloon is too much engine: stamps drawn in a burst shape (a `--stamp-shape` background), still holding the clear word.

| Standard | Themed | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Case | **Case** | Kept. "Caper" (the villains' word) was dropped: it names a crime, not a job |
| Needs you | **BATPHONE** | Clear: someone is calling you, now. Count: "1 call on the Batphone", "3 calls on the Batphone". Risk: none severe. No other Lexicon names a phone |
| Permission | **MAY I?** | Clear: a polite request, the courteous Batman. Taken questions avoided: OK TO AIR?, PERMIT?, GO AHEAD?, BLESSING?, ACCESS? |

| Stalled | **HANGING** | Clear: a hung process, and the cliffhanger. Not *dark*, *quiet* or *still*. STUCK, JAMMED, FROZEN, HELD UP, ON ICE are taken |

Soft, wide, flat cloud banks (`--clouds` `#2b4f80`, alpha 0.25–0.4) drift right to left across the upper half of the panel, about 1px every two ticks, three or four at once; behind them, fixed high in the panel, the Signal's pale ellipse with the 1966 bat (`--sky-signal`, Bone at 10%), crossed now and then by a cloud. The light is the credits' blue from above (`--haze`). Shared 8 fps clock and the usual gating; under reduced motion the Signal and one cloud stay still.
- **Engine ask:** on the `smoke` painter, a `wind` (sideways drift instead of rising) and an `aspect` (puffs wider than tall), plus a still layer drawn once (as `noise`'s still panes), for the Signal.
- **Against the neighbours:** BECOME A BAT's smoke rises from the street and fades; these clouds cross high and stay. KNIGHTMARE's ash and BLUE COLLAR's bats cross too, but as small fast marks; clouds are a few large, slow, soft shapes. TAS's blimp crosses a skyline slowly with two beams: the closest idea (one slow thing across a night sky). The difference is the Signal itself and the absence of a skyline or beams. PALE MOONLIGHT's Signature holds a small brass Signal; this one is the sky's, pale, never brass, never lit.

**A2, the Batcave's lights.** A still row of labelled consoles along the bottom [11] [12], one lamp blinking now and then. Cost: WATCHFUL PROTECTOR's lit windows (a still scene with one light switching) in another costume.

**A3, the closing credits** [18]: a black skyline with a green searchlight beam sweeping to the Signal. Cost: TAS's skyline and blimp beams, and the games' optional searchlights. Rejected.

**A4, the bat wipe.** The show's scene transition, a spinning bat emblem, crossing once a minute. Cost: jury Wave 2 §9.10 says no later Theme uses bats as weather. Listed for the maintainer, not proposed.
