# Research note: the cape of Keaton's Batman (1989, 1992)

Research note, 2026-10-13. Scope: how many points and scallops the cape of Michael Keaton's Batman shows, spread open as "the giant bat" and hanging closed, in Tim Burton's *Batman* (1989) and *Batman Returns* (1992). It feeds the PALE MOONLIGHT Theme's Bat-Clawd (`burton-1989.md` §7, `SCALLOPED` in `app/src/shared/suits.ts`). The terms used here are defined in [`CONTEXT.md`](../../../CONTEXT.md).

Provenance key used below: **Sourced** = said in so many words by a listing, article or builder. **Observed** = seen by us in photographs we downloaded and looked at (Prop Store lot galleries). **Inferred** = our reading of what the sourced and observed facts imply.

## 1. TL;DR

- **Construction: eight panels, 8 of 9 sectors of a circle.** Builders who have studied the screen-used capes describe "an eight panel cape just like the movie", laid out as a circle cut into nine sectors with one left out at the front (§2). That gives **9 radial edges, so 9 points on the hem (7 seam tips and the 2 front corners) and 8 scallops in all.**
- **Hem shape: tips down, shallow arcs up.** Each panel's hem is "a soft rounded hem; not straight but not exaggerated" (§3). On the screen-used capes the seam tips hang down as points and the panel edges curve up between them (observed). The scallops are shallow, not deep lobes.
- **Spread open, per wing: one apex, four scallops, four more points.** Each half is 4 panels. The front edge is held up on a rod, so it makes **one apex per wing** at the rod's tip, above and outboard of the hand. **No second "finger" peak rises along the top edge.** The front panel's hem drops from the apex to the outermost point, at about shoulder height. The other three panels' hems then step **diagonally down and in to the feet**, with two more tips on the way (observed in one film frame, §4).
- **Closed: the same hem.** Tips hang down at the seams, with shallow arcs between them. The current trailing and wrapped paths mostly draw it upside down: rounded lobes hang down and the cusps point up (§6).
- **1989 vs *Returns*: no difference you could see.** The *Returns* capes "were nearly identical to the original film's capes and are only distinguishable based on unseen interior details" (Prop Store, §5).
- **For the sprite:** keep the count (the current wing already has 4 scallops), but drop the second upper peak. Move the apex outboard. Turn the flat hem at y = 9.8 into a diagonal from the outer point (about y = 5.8) down to the feet (§7).

## 2. Construction: panels and points

- **Eight panels.** "It is an eight panel cape just like the movie" (KaptainKornhole, [TheRPF, "Batman '89 Cape Help"](https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/batman-89-cape-help.351590/), 24 Apr 2023). The reply gives the geometry: "If you imagine a circle divided into 9 sectors, the cape would be made up of 8 of them, with the 9th sector missing. The edges of the cape should only hang alongside the width of the chest emblem" (E Williams, same thread, 27 Apr 2023). He then tells a builder to "lay out your 9 radial points and scallops with chalk". A 2015 pattern draft counts the same 8 panels in a Prop Store photo and computes 9 sectors, leaving one out "so that there is a gap for the costume beneath to show" (BitterBits, [TheRPF, "batman 89 cape pattern"](https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/batman-89-cape-pattern.229890/), 3 Apr 2015). Both threads were read through their Wayback Machine copies; the live site returns 403.
- **How it was made.** A forum member's paraphrase of *Cinefex* #41 (Feb 1990), not checked against the issue, has the rubber "sprayed ... on this enormous table and the fabric was cut to fit each segment exactly ... so the seams just butted up together". In the same thread the shop is recalled as using "eight wedge-shaped boards" ([TheRPF, "89 Batman cape-which material was used?"](https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/89-batman-cape-which-material-was-used.64300/), seen only as a search snippet; the page could not be opened). Prop Store describes a 1989 cape as "black latex ... fabric-backed and tagged '20 Keaton' with the notation 'Best'", with a "textured surface, designed to resemble bat skin" ([Prop Store, Batsuit on display](https://propstore.com/product/batman-1989/exceptional-quality-batman-michael-keaton-batsuit-costume-on-display/)).
- **Observed on the tagged capes.** In the back views of the "20 Keaton" cape and of a *Returns* cape (Prop Store galleries, §5), we can see the centre-back seam and one seam on each side running down from the collar. That is four panels across the back, which is what an eight-panel cape shows from behind. The shoulder seams wrap out of view. This is **consistent with eight panels, not a full count.**
- **Equal panels?** Not settled. One builder thought the two back panels on a Prop Store cape looked "half the size angles of the ones either side". Others answered that "from all the photos ... the cape looks equally distributed" (toxicvenom and ohaycsa, [TheRPF pattern thread](https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/batman-89-cape-pattern.229890/), Dec 2014).
- **The collar echoes the hem.** The cowl's back flap ends in its own bat-wing edge: **four concave scallops between five points**, with the middle point down the centre-back seam (observed, both films). Builders line the cape up with "the points on the cowl" (toxicvenom, same thread). This edge belongs to the cowl, not to the cape's top edge.

## 3. Hem shape and depth

- **Sourced:** "The movie stills make it appear that each panel has a soft rounded hem; not straight but not exaggerated either" (BitterBits, [TheRPF pattern thread](https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/batman-89-cape-pattern.229890/), 2 Jan 2015). His pattern cuts "the slight curve at the bottom" of each panel and warns that if the seam allowance is cut away "it'll never come to a point at the bottom". The seams are where the points are.
- **Observed** (front and back views of the 1989 "20 Keaton" cape, and the *Returns* cape in Prop Store's Lot 29): every seam ends in a **sharp tip that hangs down**, and the edge between two tips is a **shallow arc curving up**. The arcs are not semicircles and not deep lobes. Because the latex drapes, the tips hang at slightly different heights. The two front corners hang as the longest points.
- **Depth:** no measured figure was found. A search snippet credits a "basic pattern" with "5-inch scalloped ends" on an 11-foot circle. That works out to about 13% of a panel's width, but the post itself could not be read (§8). By eye the arcs on the screen-used capes are shallow, roughly a tenth of a panel's width.
- **Length:** "the longest finished length is 51"" (BitterBits, quoting a Prop Store listing that no longer exists, §8).

## 4. Spread open: "the giant bat"

- **How it spreads.** *Returns*: "polyester lining featuring slots for posts to allow the cape to spread out rigid" ([Prop Store, Aug 2026 catalogue, Lot 29](https://content.propstore.com/auction/emlaus26-2/Extracted_Lot_PDFs/Lot_0029_pages_15-15.pdf), sourced). 1989: one builder recalls "a dramatic cape with inserts to put dowels in and spread like wings" (forum recollection, [TheRPF material thread](https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/89-batman-cape-which-material-was-used.64300/), snippet only). Another left "room in my front vertical hem to hold removable fiberglass rods for cape spreading as in the opening scene" (E Williams, [cape help thread](https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/batman-89-cape-help.351590/)). So the rods run up the **front edges**, not along every seam.
- **One film frame, observed.** The gallery of [Prop Store's 1989 Batsuit lot](https://propstore.com/product/batman-1989/exceptional-quality-batman-michael-keaton-batsuit-costume-on-display/) includes a still of Keaton standing with the cape fully spread. The frame has no caption, and the stonework suggests the opening rooftop scene (unverified). We brightened it and read it as follows:
  - **The top edge** runs taut and nearly straight from the neck to the raised hand. The hands sit at about ear-tip height. The edge carries on past the hand, along the rod, to an **apex above the head, outboard of the hand**; the right wing's apex is off the top of the frame. There is no dip and no second peak between the neck and the apex. The silhouette has no bat-wing "fingers" along its top edge.
  - **The outermost point** of each wing sits at about **shoulder height**, below the hand. Between the apex and this point, the front panel's hem makes the wing's outer edge.
  - **The lower edge** runs **diagonally from that outer point down and in to the feet**. On the right wing, the clearer one, at least two more tips show along it before it reaches the ankles. That fits a hem that steps through the remaining seams to the centre back, behind the legs.
  - The two wings are **not symmetric**: the left one is less open and folds on itself. The cape is in motion.
- **Inferred geometry.** This is what an 8-of-9-sector circle does when its front corners are lifted. The front panel swings up, so its hem becomes the outer edge from the apex to the first seam tip. The other panels' hems fall in steps toward the centre-back seam at the ankles. **Per wing: 1 apex, 4 scallops, and 4 hem points below the apex** (the first seam tip at the outside, two tips on the diagonal, and the centre back tucked behind the legs). The centre-back point is shared by the two wings.
- ***Returns* glider.** Wikipedia says the cape could "change, through use of a fold-out spring-loaded framework, into a glider" ([Batsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batsuit)). No source found gives its rib count. The dossier already dropped the glider (`burton-1989.md` §7, jury §4.1), and nothing here changes that.

## 5. 1989 vs *Batman Returns*

- **Same cape outside.** Prop Store listed a 1989 cowl and armour with a *Returns* cape and wrote: "The capes from the sequel were nearly identical to the original film's capes and are only distinguishable based on unseen interior details" ([Prop Store, Lot 29](https://propstore.com/product/batman-1989/lot-29-batmans-michael-keaton-batsuit-costume/)). The 1989 cape is latex with a fabric backing ("20 Keaton", above). The *Returns* cape is "an original latex cape with fabric lining", "polyester lining featuring slots for posts" ([Lot 29 PDF](https://content.propstore.com/auction/emlaus26-2/Extracted_Lot_PDFs/Lot_0029_pages_15-15.pdf)).
- **Observed.** The *Returns* cape's hem (tips down at the seams, shallow arcs between) and its collar (four scallops between five points) match the 1989 cape.
- **Fastening changed.** *Returns* added "a mechanical system of bolts and spikes beneath the breast plate to secure the cowl and cape", because "if [Keaton] turned around quickly the cape would stay where it was" (Fennell 1992, as cited in [Special effects of *Batman Returns*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_effects_of_Batman_Returns)). This changes how the cape hangs, not its outline.
- **Many variants in 1989.** "25 different cape looks" were made (AMC blog 2007, as cited in [*Batman* (1989 film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_(1989_film))). A builder recalls a lighter stunt cape, a "dramatic" cape for spreading, and the acting cape (forum, snippet only). These describe design trials and stunt copies, not a different hem.

## 6. The closed and trailing hem in the current sprite

- `SCALLOPED.wingLeft` already bows its arcs **upward**: the control points at y = 8.8 sit above the endpoints at y = 9.8, so the tips point down. That matches the film's orientation.
- `trailA` and `trailB` put every control point **below** the chord (`Q12.2 10.9 11 10.4`, `Q9 11.2 7 10`, ...). That draws rounded lobes hanging down, with the cusps pointing up between them, which is the reverse of the cape.
- `wrapped`, `nearFront` and `farBehind` **alternate** up and down arcs, so they read as a wave rather than as points.
- **Closed, the hem is the same kind as spread** (§3): tips down at the seams, shallow arcs up between them. It is not a row of rounded lobes. The dossier's phrase "rounded scallops" (`burton-1989.md` §7) holds only if it means the shallow arcs *between* pointed tips.
- **The trailing paths also break the dossier's own spec.** `burton-1989.md` §7 already asks for quadratic arcs "curving up between points". `trailA` and `trailB` curve down, so the fix below brings them back in line with §7; it is not a new rule. The item "the 1989 cape's hem shape" in `burton-1989.md` §12 can now move from unverified to sourced, with this note as the reference.

## 7. Recommendation for the pixel silhouette

Coordinates are in the sprite's grid (body x = 3–13, shoulders y = 5, feet y = 11, viewBox x −6..22, y −1..12; `mirrored` flips about x = 8 via `translate(16 0) scale(-1 1)`). The arms-up pose has the gloves at (1, 3) and (14, 3), 1×1.

**Spread wing (`wingLeft`), per wing:**

- **Upper points: 1.** One apex at the rod's tip, at the maintainer's y = 1.4, moved **outboard** to x ≈ −2.4. The frame actually puts the apex higher, above the head. y = 1.4 is the maintainer's choice, not the film's. For more fidelity, lift the apex toward y ≈ 0 (the viewBox allows −1, and `burton-1989.md` §7 first proposed −1) and leave the rest of the path as it is. The top edge runs from the shoulder through the hand to the apex: a straight line from (3.4, 5.2) to (1.2, 3), which crosses the glove so the hand grips the edge, then on at a shallower angle to the apex. **Drop the second peak at (−3.6, 2.4) and the notch at (−1.4, 3.6).** Nothing in the sources or the frame supports a bat-finger top edge.
- **Hem points: 4 below the apex.** The outer point at (−5.4, 5.8), about shoulder height and the wing's widest point. Two tips on the diagonal, at (−2.2, 9.6) and (0.8, 10.4). The centre back tucked behind the legs and the cape's `back` panel at (3.4, 11).
- **Scallops: 4**, one per panel. Each is a shallow arc bowing toward the wing's inside, with a sagitta of about 0.55–0.6 cells. That is deeper than the film's roughly 10%, a compromise so they read at 54 px, not a measurement. The tips stay sharp, made where two arcs meet.
- **Corners per wing: 6 instead of 9.** The current outline has two peaks, a notch, an outer corner and five hem points; the proposed one has an apex, a bend at the hand and four hem points.

Proposed `wingLeft`:

```
M3.4 5.2 L1.2 3 L-2.4 1.4 Q-3 4.2 -5.4 5.8 Q-2.9 7 -2.2 9.6 Q-0.4 8.8 0.8 10.4 Q2.2 9.5 3.4 11 Z
```

We drew this next to the current path with a rough copy of the moon outfit map (scratch render, 24 px and 2–3 px per cell). It stays inside the viewBox. Its inner edge lands under the body and the `back` panel (x = 3.4–12.6, y = 5–11.4). Even at 2–3 px per cell, below the header's roughly 4–4.6 px per cell at 54–60 px, the silhouette reads as one high point and a long scalloped lower edge on each side. Check it on the real window before committing (the Wave's 60 px check). If the lower scallops vanish at that size, deepen them toward 0.8 rather than flattening the diagonal.

**Closed and trailing hem** (it differs from what is drawn now, §6). Rule: tips hang down at about a 2-cell pitch. Between them, arcs curve **up**, with the control point about 1 cell above the chord's midpoint (a sagitta of about 0.5). Stagger the tips' heights by ±0.2–0.3 for drape. Worked examples:

- `wrapped` (7 tips, 6 scallops):
  `M2.6 6.4 L13.4 6.4 L14.2 10.6 Q13.3 9.7 12.2 10.3 Q11.2 9.6 10.1 10.6 Q9 9.8 8 10.4 Q7 9.8 5.9 10.6 Q4.8 9.6 3.8 10.3 Q2.7 9.7 1.8 10.6 Z`
- `trailA`: the same tips as today, with the arcs flipped:
  `M3 5 L13 5 L13.4 9.2 Q11.8 8.9 11 10.4 Q9 9.3 7 10 Q5.1 9.5 3 10.8 Q0.9 9.5 -1 10 Q-2.9 9.5 -4.6 10.8 L-3.6 8.8 L-5.6 7.4 L-1.4 6.6 Z`
- `trailB`, `nearFront` and `farBehind`: apply the same flip (every hem `Q` control point above its chord). `trailA` keeps its 4-cell pitch; adding a tip between each pair would bring it to the dossier's "about 2 cells per scallop".

**Decided on screen (2026-10-13):** the maintainer picked the spread wing with its apex lifted above his head (y = 0, variant R2), over the same wing at the ears' height (R1) and the earlier two-peak wing (B); the closed and trailing hems take the tips-down rule above.

## 8. Unverified

- **The panel count** rests on builders (TheRPF, 2014–2023), on a recollection of *Cinefex* #41 that was not read, and on our partial seam count in two back views. No costume maker's own count was found. Bob Ringwood's and Vin Burnham's accounts (*Batman: The Official Book of the Movie*, *Cinefex* #41) and anything by Jose Fernandez or Steve Wang for *Returns* were not reachable.
- **The spread silhouette** comes from **one** uncaptioned, compressed film frame, brightened by us. The cape is moving, the two wings are asymmetric, the right wing's apex is off-frame, and the scene is not confirmed. A second frame, or a press still of the spread cape, would settle the apex's height and the number of tips along the diagonal.
- **The apex above the hand on a rod:** the 1989 rods are a builder's recollection. The *Returns* "slots for posts" are sourced, but where they sit (front edges only?) is not said.
- **Scallop depth:** no measurement. The "5-inch scalloped ends" figure comes from a search snippet of a post we could not open. The 0.55–0.6-cell sagitta is a legibility choice.
- **The 51" length** cites a Prop Store listing ("batman-michael-keaton-batsuit-pieces") that is gone; its archive copy could not be fetched (the Wayback Machine was rate-limiting and then offline).
- **Equal or unequal panels:** builders disagree (§2).
- **batmannews.de**, Wikipedia's source for the suit pages, is excluded from the Wayback Machine.
- **Heritage, Julien's, Christie's and Profiles in History:** no cape lot from these houses was found that describes the hem. Profiles in History sold a *Returns* suit in 2008 (reported only by price).
- **No laid-flat photo** of a screen-used cape was found. Every observation here is of a cape on a form or in motion.
